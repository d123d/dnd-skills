# Fantasy Fiction Writing & World Craft

Practical craft contract for writing and revising English fantasy fiction that feels lived-in, concrete, and free of generic AI fantasy clichés. Pairs cleanly with `worldbuilding`, `scene-craft`, `character-management`, `systemic-worldbuilding`, `dialogue`, and `fantasy-fiction-writer`.

## 1. Worldbuilding That Reads Like Life, Not a Textbook

- **Lived-in rules over lore dumps:** Show magic, currency, food, road danger, law, and superstition through how ordinary people adjust their daily habits—never pause a chapter for three paragraphs of encyclopedia history.
- **Magic needs cost, limit, and friction:** Whatever power characters wield, define what it **cannot** fix, what it **consumes** (time, blood, guild favors, memory, rare materials), and what secondary economy or fear it creates.
- **Avoid monolithic cultures:** No planet-of-hats kingdoms or species where everyone shares one accent, alignment, and job. Give every region local class tension, dialect drift, and competing faiths.
- **Consistent naming phonetics:** Pick 3–4 repeatable consonant/vowel habits per culture rather than random apostrophe salad (*Kael'thas'rynn*).

## 2. Deep Limited POV & Sensory Filtering

- **One POV per scene.** Everything on the page is noticed and interpreted through that character's training and scars:
  - A mason notices mortar rot, cracked lintels, and siege weak points.
  - A hedge-mage notices ozone smell, chalk residue, and ward fatigue.
  - An urchin notices belt-pouch weight, boot leather, and escape alleys.
- **No head-hopping or floating camera.** Never describe facial expressions the POV character cannot see on their own face without a mirror or touch.
- **Earn revelations through action:** Reveal backstory inside present stakes—a flinch, a debt collector's mark, a half-forgotten prayer.

## 3. Pacing, Dialogue, & Action Craft

- **Scene -> Sequel rhythm:** High-tension action/conflict scene (`Goal -> Conflict -> Outcome: Yes-but / No-and`) followed by brief sensory fallout and hard decision (`Reaction -> Dilemma -> Decision`).
- **Combat is spatial and costly:** Track footing, light, armor weight, breath, distance, and panic rather than weightless anime choreography. Hits bruise, slow arms down, and ruin plans.
- **Dialogue carries subtext:** People rarely state their exact thesis. Let characters deflect, negotiate status, lie by omission, and answer the question behind the question.

## 4. Anti-AI Fantasy Prose Audit (Run Before Finalizing)

Hunt down and rewrite every instance of these common AI tells:

| Tell | Why It Hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Stock fantasy filler (*ancient tapestry, weight of destiny, obsidian darkness, ozone and magic, hummed with arcane energy*) | Signals synthetic boilerplate instantly | Replace with specific physical texture, tool, smell, or weather |
| Emotion labels (*felt a surge of dread, eyes flashed with determination*) | Tells instead of showing | Show concrete body response, breath, grip, or avoidance |
| "Not X, but Y" & stacked em-dash pairs in every paragraph | Uniform cadence puts readers to sleep | Vary sentence length; commit to one direct image |
| One-line dramatic trailer closers (*Nothing would ever be the same.*) | Melodramatic emptiness | End the beat on a physical detail, choice, or spoken line |
| Echoing summaries (*With newfound resolve, they stepped into the unknown.*) | Restates what the scene already showed | Cut the last sentence; stop one beat earlier |
